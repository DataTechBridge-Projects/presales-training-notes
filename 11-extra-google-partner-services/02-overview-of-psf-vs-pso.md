# Overview of PSF vs PSO
{: .no_toc }

*Read duration: 2:04*

**PSF (Partner Sales Fund)** and **PSO (Partner Solutions Organization)** are both Google-funded programs, but they sit at different points in the deal lifecycle and fund different kinds of work. Confusing the two in front of a client — or in an internal funding request — is one of the fastest ways to get a request bounced back for rework.

PSF is pre-sales, deal-acceleration money: it funds the activities that get a specific, registered opportunity across the line — architecture workshops, a scoped proof-of-concept, a migration assessment, or a business-case workshop with the client's economic buyer. It's tied tightly to an opportunity in Google's pipeline view and has a comparatively fast approval cycle because the ask is narrow and the payoff — a closed deal — is easy for Google to justify.

PSO, by contrast, is delivery and enablement money that shows up once a deal is closed or nearly so — it funds hands-on technical delivery, training, and solution-building resources that help you actually implement the workload successfully, which in turn protects the client's GCP consumption, and Google's revenue, long after the sale closes.

| | PSF (Partner Sales Fund) | PSO (Partner Solutions Organization) |
|---|---|---|
| Deal stage | Pre-sales, opportunity in pipeline | Post-sale or late-stage, near-committed |
| Funds | Workshops, PoCs, assessments | Delivery, migration, enablement resources |
| Goal | Win the deal | Ensure successful, sticky delivery |
| Approval speed | Faster, opportunity-scoped | Slower, delivery-plan-scoped |

{: .key-term }
> Think of **PSF as "help me win it"** and **PSO as "help me deliver it well."** Pitching a PSO-shaped ask, like a full migration team, as a PSF request is a common rejection reason.

In practice, a well-run deal often uses both in sequence: PSF funds the proof-of-concept that proves feasibility and closes the opportunity, then PSO funds the delivery team that executes the resulting SOW. Knowing which bucket a request belongs in, before you submit it, saves a review cycle and keeps your Google partner manager relationship credible.
