# GCP Case Studies with PSF/PSO
{: .no_toc }

*Read duration: 2:11*

The clearest way to understand PSF/PSO funding is to see how it changes deal economics in practice. The scenarios below are composites drawn from common patterns across GCP migration deals, not verbatim case studies of named clients, but they reflect the kind of math you should expect to run yourself.

**Scenario 1: The stalled proof-of-concept.** A mid-market retailer was evaluating a move from an on-prem data warehouse to BigQuery but couldn't justify the internal engineering time for a proof-of-concept without some evidence it would work. A PSF request funded a two-week architecture workshop and a scoped PoC migrating a single high-value dataset. The PoC came in fast enough that the client's data team saw query performance gains firsthand, which turned a stalled six-month evaluation into a signed SOW within five weeks, and removed the PoC cost entirely from the deal's opening price conversation.

**Scenario 2: The de-risked lift-and-shift.** A logistics company had budget approved for a GKE migration but was worried about downtime risk during cutover. PSO funding covered a dedicated migration engineer alongside the delivery team for the first month, which meant the client got senior hands-on support without the partner having to eat that cost inside a fixed-fee SOW. The result was a cleaner cutover, a reference-able client, and a delivery margin that stayed intact because the extra resourcing didn't come out of the contracted fee.

**Scenario 3: The competitive save.** A healthcare software vendor was comparing GCP against a competing cloud with a steeper discount on paper. A PSF-funded business-case workshop reframed the comparison around total migration cost and time-to-value rather than list price, and the funded workshop itself became evidence of Google's commitment to the account, enough to close the deal without matching the competitor's discount.

Across all three, the pattern repeats: the funding doesn't replace your proposal's value story, it removes a specific cost or risk objection that would otherwise stall it. Learn to identify which objection you're facing early in discovery, and you'll know which program to reach for before the client even raises it.
