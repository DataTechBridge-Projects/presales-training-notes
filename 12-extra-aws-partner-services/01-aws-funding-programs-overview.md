# AWS Funding Programs Overview
{: .no_toc }

*Read duration: 6:28*

AWS runs a portfolio of **partner funding programs** designed to lower the cost and risk of a customer trying, moving to, or expanding on AWS. As a pre-sales professional, these programs are not a footnote you mention after the deal is won — they're often the difference between a customer who stalls at "we need budget approval" and one who moves forward now, because AWS is picking up part of the tab. Knowing which program applies to which deal shape, and when in the sales cycle to invoke it, is a genuine differentiator against competitors who don't bring funding into the conversation at all.

## POC Funding

**Proof of Concept (POC) funding** exists for the earliest, most uncertain stage of a deal: when a customer is technically curious but not yet convinced enough to commit real budget. AWS will fund a defined, time-boxed technical validation — think a two-to-six week engagement proving that a specific workload, architecture pattern, or migration approach actually works for that customer's environment. This is the program you reach for when a stakeholder says "I like the idea, but I need to see it work before I take this to my CFO." The funding typically covers AWS usage costs and, depending on the program variant, some partner services time, but it always requires a clearly scoped success criteria defined up front — AWS won't fund an open-ended experiment.

## Migration Acceleration Program (MAP)

The **Migration Acceleration Program (MAP)** is built for a different moment in the deal: the customer has already decided to move workloads to AWS, and the conversation has shifted from "should we" to "how do we fund and de-risk the move." MAP funding scales with migration size and complexity, and it's structured around a three-phase model — assess, mobilize, and migrate — with funding available at each phase. In practice, you'll invoke MAP once a customer has a real migration scope (a data center exit, an application portfolio assessment, a mainframe modernization) and needs help justifying the business case internally. MAP credits can offset dual-running costs during cutover, which is frequently the line item that kills migration budgets when it's not accounted for.

## Microsoft Modernization Program (MMP)

The **Microsoft Modernization Program (MMP)** is a narrower, purpose-built incentive for customers running Windows Server, SQL Server, or .NET workloads who are looking to modernize onto AWS-native or open-source equivalents rather than simply lift-and-shift. This program matters in pre-sales conversations where licensing cost is the customer's real pain point — a customer facing a Microsoft licensing renewal or an end-of-support deadline is a strong MMP candidate. MMP funding can offset the cost of re-platforming SQL Server to Amazon Aurora or RDS, or containerizing .NET applications, and it's often the financial hook that turns a defensive "we have to move because support is ending" conversation into a proactive modernization deal.

## Generative AI Incentives

AWS also offers **generative AI incentive funding** aimed at customers building or piloting GenAI use cases on Amazon Bedrock, SageMaker, or related services. These incentives typically fund proof-of-value engagements — a customer testing a specific use case like document summarization, a customer service copilot, or a code assistant — where the customer needs to prove business value before committing to production-scale spend. This is the fastest-moving category of the four, with eligibility criteria that shift as AWS iterates on its GenAI portfolio, so it's worth confirming current terms with your AWS Partner Development Manager (PDM) rather than assuming last quarter's rules still apply.

## Comparing the Programs

| Program | Typical Use Case | What It Funds | When to Invoke |
|---|---|---|---|
| POC Funding | Early technical validation, no committed budget yet | AWS usage, limited partner hours | Customer is curious but unconvinced |
| MAP | Full workload or data center migration | Assessment, dual-running costs, migration tooling | Customer has committed to migrating, needs business case |
| MMP | Windows/SQL/.NET modernization off Microsoft licensing | Re-platforming and containerization costs | Licensing renewal or support deadline is the trigger |
| GenAI Incentives | Piloting Bedrock/SageMaker use cases | Proof-of-value engagements | Customer wants to test GenAI value before scaling |

{: .important }
> All four programs require a signed AWS partner agreement and a defined scope **before** the engagement starts — you cannot retroactively apply for funding on work that's already underway. Loop in your AWS Partner Development Manager during scoping, not after you've already quoted the deal.

The common thread across all four programs is timing: funding has to be requested and approved before the work begins, which means it belongs in your scoping and proposal conversations, not as an afterthought once the SOW is signed. When you're building a proposal and sense budget hesitation, pause and ask which of these four programs actually fits the customer's situation — POC funding for the unconvinced, MAP for committed migrations, MMP for licensing-driven modernization, and GenAI incentives for AI pilots. Bringing the right program into the conversation at the right moment signals to the customer that you're not just selling a services engagement, you're bringing AWS's balance sheet to help them get there.
