# Proposal Writing & Estimation

Ten multiple-choice questions covering proposal structure, scope and risk framing, estimation approaches, and pricing models from this section.

**1. What should a proposal generally lead with instead of a company overview or "About Us" section?**
A. A list of past clients
B. The client's problem, restated in their own language
C. The full pricing table
D. A description of your delivery methodology

**2. Why should pricing typically appear after the proposed approach and scope, rather than at the front of the document?**
A. Procurement requires it by law
B. It gives the reader context to judge the price against value rather than in isolation
C. Pricing tables take longer to prepare
D. It keeps the executive summary shorter

**3. In a proposal, what is the primary purpose of explicitly stating what is out of scope?**
A. To make the document longer and more thorough-looking
B. To prevent the client from assuming an unstated item is included
C. To reduce the number of deliverables listed
D. To satisfy a legal requirement in every contract

**4. How do deliverables differ from scope in a proposal?**
A. Deliverables are the tangible, verifiable artifacts that scope produces
B. Deliverables and scope mean the same thing
C. Deliverables only apply to fixed-price engagements
D. Scope is defined after deliverables are agreed

**5. What is the key difference between an assumption and a risk in a proposal?**
A. Assumptions are legal terms; risks are technical terms
B. An assumption is a condition the estimate depends on being true; a risk is something that could go wrong even if assumptions hold
C. Risks only appear in fixed-price proposals
D. There is no meaningful difference

**6. What does analogous estimation rely on to produce a number?**
A. A detailed task-by-task breakdown of the current project
B. Comparison to a similar past engagement
C. A fixed percentage markup on materials cost
D. The client's stated budget ceiling

**7. What is the main advantage of building a work breakdown structure (WBS) before estimating?**
A. It removes the need for any contingency buffer
B. It decomposes the engagement into tasks, producing a more defensible, explainable total
C. It guarantees the project will finish under budget
D. It replaces the need for a proposal's scope section

**8. Why should an estimate include a contingency buffer?**
A. To inflate the price and increase margin regardless of risk
B. To absorb the unknowns that surface once work starts, sized to match the project's assumptions and risks
C. Because clients expect all quotes to be rounded up
D. Buffers are only required for T&M engagements

**9. Which pricing model places the most estimation risk on the vendor?**
A. Time & Materials
B. Retainer
C. Fixed price
D. All models place equal risk on the vendor

**10. What condition must hold for outcome-based pricing to be workable?**
A. The client must prepay the full engagement cost
B. The vendor must control enough of the variables to actually influence or guarantee the outcome
C. The scope must be left completely undefined
D. The engagement must last less than one month

## Answer Key

1. **B** — Leading with the client's problem in their own words proves you listened; opening with your company signals a generic pitch.
2. **B** — Presenting approach and scope first lets the reader evaluate price against demonstrated value instead of negotiating a number in a vacuum.
3. **B** — An unstated exclusion is one the client will reasonably assume is included, which is exactly the gap that creates disputes later.
4. **A** — Deliverables are the specific, verifiable artifacts scope produces, not the scope statement itself.
5. **B** — Assumptions are conditions the estimate depends on; risks are things that could still go wrong even when those conditions are met.
6. **B** — Analogous estimation compares the current opportunity to a similar past engagement rather than building a fresh breakdown.
7. **B** — A WBS breaks work into discrete tasks you can estimate and sum, so the total can be defended line by line.
8. **B** — A contingency buffer exists to absorb the unknowns that inevitably surface, and its size should scale with the project's real risk.
9. **C** — Fixed price quotes a single number for defined scope, so any estimation error becomes cost the vendor absorbs.
10. **B** — Outcome-based pricing only works when the vendor has enough control over the relevant variables to reliably deliver the measured result.
