# Welcome to the Course
{: .no_toc }

*Read duration: 2:20*

**Pre-sales** is the work that happens between "a prospect is interested" and "a prospect signs" — and it's where deals are actually won or lost. Sales gets the meeting; pre-sales proves the fit, shapes the scope, prices the work, and writes the documents that hold up when the project starts. If you've ever watched a promising deal stall because nobody could answer "what exactly are we building, and what will it cost," this guide is about closing that gap.

This course is built as a field guide, not a lecture series to memorize and forget. It starts with the fundamentals — what pre-sales is, where it sits relative to sales and delivery, and the vocabulary you'll use every day. From there it moves into **RFx Essentials**, covering how RFIs, RFQs, and RFPs actually work and how to respond to each without wasting a week on the wrong one. **Discovery & Requirements Gathering** teaches you how to run a client conversation that surfaces real pain instead of a wish list, including two role-play scenarios so you can hear what a hard conversation sounds like before you're in one. **Core Contract Documents** and **Proposal Writing & Estimation** get you comfortable with NDAs, MSAs, SOWs, and the pricing models that go into a proposal that survives legal review. Later sections cover **Winning Enterprise Demos**, keeping delivery and stakeholders aligned through **Collaboration, Tools, and Change Control**, and the sharper judgment calls in **Advanced Strategies & Customer Types** — plus extra sections on using AI in pre-sales work, partner programs from Google, AWS, and Azure, and the practical realities of business travel.

None of this is theoretical. Every section pulls from patterns that show up repeatedly in real enterprise deals — the same traps, the same clauses that save you later, the same discovery questions that separate a good scope from a painful one. Work through the sections in order the first time; after that, use it as a reference you come back to before your next call, your next SOW, or your next proposal deadline.
