# Meet Your Instructor
{: .no_toc }

*Read duration: 0:48*

The perspective behind this guide comes from years spent on the pre-sales side of enterprise deals — not observing from the sidelines, but sitting in the discovery calls, writing the SOWs, defending the estimates, and standing in the room when a client's legal team pushed back on a clause at 5pm the day before signature. That's the vantage point this course is written from: **what actually happens**, not what a textbook says should happen.

Most of that experience comes from working with global clients across industries — organizations with their own procurement processes, their own risk tolerances, and their own definitions of "done." Some engagements started as a clean RFP response; others began as a single conversation with a stakeholder who wasn't sure what they needed yet. Some were straightforward fixed-price builds; others required walking a client through why a **time-and-materials** model made more sense than the fixed bid they'd originally asked for. That range matters, because pre-sales work rarely looks the same twice, and a guide built around one tidy scenario would leave you unprepared for the messier, more common ones.

A recurring theme across those engagements — and across this course — is that the technical content of a deal (the architecture, the feature list, the demo) is rarely what breaks it. What breaks deals is ambiguity: a scope that was never pinned down, a contract clause nobody read closely, a stakeholder whose real concern never got asked about. This guide focuses heavily on the judgment calls and the documents — the SOW language, the discovery questions, the pricing comparisons — because that's where the real risk lives, and where a little preparation goes a long way.

This course won't turn every scenario into a script. Instead, it aims to build the **instincts and vocabulary** you need to handle the scenario that shows up next — the one nobody wrote a template for. The lessons ahead are organized to build on each other, but they're also written to stand alone as reference material once you're back on a live deal.
