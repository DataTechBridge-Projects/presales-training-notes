# RFx Essentials — Test Your Knowledge

Nine questions covering RFI/RFQ/RFP fundamentals, informal deal paths, reading and responding to client documents, and common RFx pitfalls.

**1. A customer document asks broad questions about your company's capabilities and references, with no request for pricing. What type of document is this most likely to be?**

A. RFQ
B. RFP
C. RFI
D. SOW

**2. A customer sends a document with a fully defined scope and asks vendors to submit itemized pricing for comparison. This is best described as a:**

A. RFI
B. RFQ
C. RFP
D. NDA

**3. Which statement correctly distinguishes an RFP from an RFQ?**

A. An RFP only evaluates price; an RFQ evaluates approach and team
B. An RFP evaluates approach, fit, and price together; an RFQ is priced against an already-fixed scope
C. An RFP is issued only after a contract is signed
D. An RFQ is always longer than an RFP

**4. A customer emails your champion asking "can you send us something like this" with no formal process attached. What's the recommended first move?**

A. Immediately send your standard capabilities deck
B. Quote a firm price to move fast before the customer talks to a competitor
C. Run a lightweight discovery pass to confirm problem, budget owner, timeline, and sign-off chain
D. Wait for the customer to formalize the process before responding at all

**5. Why is it risky to skip discovery on an informal, non-RFx opportunity?**

A. Informal opportunities never turn into real deals
B. The absence of a formal process means you have to build the structure yourself, or the deal drifts without a clear path to close
C. Discovery is only required when a legal document is involved
D. It isn't risky — informal deals close faster without discovery

**6. In a client RFP, a requirement is phrased "must support single sign-on." How should this phrasing be interpreted?**

A. As a soft preference that can be addressed later
B. As a hard gate — failing to meet it can disqualify your response
C. As boilerplate that evaluators typically ignore
D. As a request for pricing only

**7. You notice an RFP's technical requirements are oddly specific and match one competitor's product almost exactly. What does this most likely signal?**

A. The customer has no real requirements at all
B. The document is actually an RFI in disguise
C. An incumbent vendor may be favored and the process could be a compliance formality
D. The customer made a clerical error and the RFP should be ignored

**8. When formatting an RFP response, what approach do evaluators generally reward?**

A. Reorganizing the customer's structure into a more creative format
B. Mirroring the customer's own requested structure and numbering so answers are easy to find
C. Combining all answers into a single narrative section
D. Omitting sections the customer didn't emphasize

**9. A response answers a rollback-plan requirement with only "rollback procedures will be documented during planning." What is the main problem with this answer?**

A. It admits a gap, which automatically disqualifies the bid
B. It's too long and repeats the requirement
C. It gives no real detail, so it reads as if the vendor hasn't actually thought through failure scenarios
D. It should have included pricing instead

**10. A strong, well-argued RFP response gets disqualified before scoring because it used the wrong file format and missed the submission deadline. What does this illustrate?**

A. Compliance formalities (signatures, formats, deadlines) are enforced literally and can eliminate a response regardless of content quality
B. Procurement teams always make exceptions for strong content
C. File format only matters for RFQs, not RFPs
D. Deadlines are flexible as long as the content is strong

## Answer Key

1. **C** — No pricing requested and broad capability/reference questions are the hallmark of an RFI.
2. **B** — A fully defined scope with a request for comparable pricing is an RFQ.
3. **B** — An RFP scores approach, fit, and price together; an RFQ prices an already-fixed scope.
4. **C** — Informal opportunities still require you to confirm the fundamentals discovery would normally surface.
5. **B** — Without a formal process, you have to supply the structure yourself or risk the deal stalling.
6. **B** — "Must support" language is a hard gate that can disqualify a response if unmet.
7. **C** — Oddly specific requirements matching one product often mean an incumbent is favored and the RFP is a compliance exercise.
8. **B** — Mirroring the customer's requested structure makes evaluators' scoring job easier and is rewarded.
9. **C** — A vague answer with no mechanism detailed signals the vendor hasn't planned for failure, regardless of technical truth.
10. **A** — Missed formalities can disqualify a response before a single evaluator reads the content.
