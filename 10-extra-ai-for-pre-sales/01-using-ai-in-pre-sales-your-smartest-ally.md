# Using AI in Pre-Sales — Your Smartest Ally
{: .no_toc }

*Read duration: 3:20*

A pre-sales cycle runs on two things you never have enough of: time and information. You're expected to walk into a discovery call sounding like you've followed an account for months, turn a scope conversation into a polished proposal in days, and defend an estimate built from a client's half-formed requirements — all while running two or three other opportunities in parallel. That's exactly the kind of workload where **AI**, used well, stops being a novelty and becomes the closest thing you have to a force multiplier on your desk.

Four areas of pre-sales are where it earns its keep fastest.

**Research** is the easiest win. Before a discovery call, you can point an AI tool at a prospect's public filings, press releases, recent job postings, and product pages and get back a structured briefing — likely tech stack, recent leadership changes, stated strategic priorities — in minutes instead of the hour or two it would otherwise take to piece together by hand. You still verify anything you plan to say out loud in the room, but you walk in already informed instead of starting from zero.

**Proposal drafting** is where the time savings compound. Feed an AI tool your discovery notes, a past proposal as a style reference, and the client's stated requirements, and it will produce a genuinely useful first draft of an executive summary or scope narrative — not final copy, but a real starting point that gets you past the blank page and lets you spend your limited hours on the parts that actually win the deal: pricing strategy, differentiation, and risk language.

**Estimation** benefits in a quieter but more valuable way. Feed a draft scope or requirements list to an AI tool alongside the shape of a past, similar SOW, and ask it to flag anything that looks missing — a deliverable mentioned in discovery notes but absent from the scope, an assumption that was implied but never written down. Catching a gap here, before the SOW is signed, is far cheaper than catching it as a change order six weeks into delivery.

**Opportunity management** rounds it out — summarizing long CRM histories before a call, drafting follow-up emails, or flagging deals that have gone quiet longer than they should have, so your attention goes where the pipeline actually needs it instead of wherever you happened to look last.

{: .important }
> AI tools remember what you paste into them. Never put confidential client data, unreleased pricing, or anything covered by an NDA into a public AI tool that isn't covered by your organization's own data-handling agreement — check with your team before you paste, not after.

None of this replaces judgment — an AI draft is a starting point you edit, not a final answer you ship. The rest of this section builds on that idea: a full walkthrough of turning an RFP into a clickable prototype, a framework for spotting and scoping AI-specific opportunities, the vocabulary you need to sound credible in front of a technical buyer, and a way to think about where AI actually creates value instead of just noise.
