# Azure Funding Programs Overview
{: .no_toc }

*Read duration: 3:42*

Microsoft funds Azure deals the same way Google and AWS fund theirs: by underwriting the parts of a sale a customer won't pay for out of pocket — an architecture assessment, a proof-of-concept build, a migration sprint. The mechanics differ by program, but the goal is always the same: get real, working proof in front of the customer before they've signed anything, so the business case sells itself. Three programs do most of the work — **ECIF**, **AMM**, and **Azure Innovate** — and knowing which one fits your deal, and when to ask for it, matters more than memorizing eligibility rules.

## ECIF (End Customer Investment Funds)

**ECIF** is Microsoft's general-purpose, deal-specific investment fund. Unlike a fixed program with a published rate card, ECIF is discretionary money a Microsoft account team can request for a named customer opportunity — covering things like partner delivery hours for a proof-of-concept, a workshop, migration labor, or even third-party tooling costs tied to closing a specific deal. Because it's tied to an opportunity rather than a workload category, ECIF is the fund you reach for when a deal doesn't cleanly fit AMM or Azure Innovate's structured scope, or when you need funding fast and the standard programs' intake process is too slow.

{: .important }
> ECIF has to be approved through the Microsoft account team **before** the work starts — you cannot do the engagement first and request reimbursement after. Loop in your Microsoft seller early, as soon as you can name the opportunity and rough scope.

## AMM (Azure Migrate and Modernize)

**AMM** is Microsoft's structured program for moving existing workloads — VMs, databases, application servers — onto Azure. It funds the full migration arc: assessment and business-case building, migration execution, and post-migration optimization, with partner-delivered offers pre-approved for funding at each stage. AMM is the right call once a customer has a concrete migration target (a data center exit, an end-of-support deadline, a lift-and-shift of a legacy estate) but needs help building the cost and risk case, or funding to offset the labor of the move itself.

## Azure Innovate

**Azure Innovate** is AMM's counterpart for net-new workloads rather than migrations — funding pilots and builds in application modernization and data-and-AI scenarios. Where AMM answers "how do we move what we already run," Azure Innovate answers "how do we build something new on Azure," funding proof-of-concept builds, hackathon-style envisioning sessions, and MVP development for things like a modern app rebuild or an analytics/AI platform. Invoke it when the conversation shifts from lift-and-shift to a customer asking what's possible on the platform.

| Program | Funds | Invoke when |
|---|---|---|
| ECIF | Deal-specific costs — POCs, workshops, delivery hours | Deal doesn't fit a structured program, or you need funding fast |
| AMM | Assess, migrate, optimize existing workloads | Customer has a concrete migration target |
| Azure Innovate | New app or data/AI builds | Conversation is about building new, not moving old |

In practice these stack across a deal's lifecycle: ECIF gets a stalled conversation moving with a quick workshop, AMM funds the migration once the target workload is defined, and Azure Innovate carries the customer into modernization once the base estate is settled. Bring your Microsoft account team in early on all three — none of them are self-service, and the approval lag is the single biggest reason partners miss out on funding they were otherwise eligible for.
