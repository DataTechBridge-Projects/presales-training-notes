# What is Pre-Sales and Why It Matters
{: .no_toc }

*Read duration: 1:36*

**Pre-sales** is the work that happens between a client expressing interest and a contract getting signed — and, just as importantly, between a contract getting signed and a delivery team being able to execute it without surprises. It's the technical and commercial bridge between what a client says they need and what actually gets built, priced, and delivered. If sales is about winning trust and closing the deal, pre-sales is about making sure the deal that gets closed is one the delivery organization can actually stand behind.

In practice, that means a pre-sales professional sits in on discovery calls translating vague business pain into concrete requirements, sizes up whether a request for proposal is even winnable before the team invests weeks in a response, builds the solution architecture and estimate that back up a price, and reviews the statement of work line by line so it doesn't quietly commit the company to something nobody scoped. You're the person in the room who has to say "yes, and here's exactly what that costs and how long it takes" — not just "yes."

Why it matters is simple: **deals that are won badly get delivered badly**. A proposal that overpromises scope to win the deal doesn't disappear once the ink dries — it becomes the delivery team's problem, then the client's disappointment, then a renewal that doesn't happen. Pre-sales exists to prevent that gap between what was sold and what can be delivered. A strong pre-sales function catches ambiguous requirements before they become disputed change orders, catches unrealistic timelines before they become missed milestones, and catches scope creep before it becomes a margin problem.

{: .important }
> Pre-sales isn't "sales support" — it's the function that determines whether a signed deal is actually deliverable. Treat every commitment you help write as one your delivery team will have to live with.
