# MSA – Key Clauses & Real Examples
{: .no_toc }

*Read duration: 1:58*

The **Master Service Agreement (MSA)** is the backbone of the client relationship — the legal document that sets terms once so that every future project can move faster. Instead of renegotiating liability caps, payment terms, and IP ownership on every engagement, the MSA locks those terms in up front, and each individual project is then scoped through a much shorter **SOW** that references back to it. If the NDA is what lets you talk, the MSA is what lets you work.

A few clauses show up in nearly every MSA and are worth being able to recognize on sight:

- **Limitation of liability** — caps how much either party can be sued for, often expressed as a multiple of fees paid (e.g., *"Vendor's total liability under this Agreement shall not exceed the fees paid in the twelve (12) months preceding the claim"*). This is frequently the most negotiated clause in the whole document.
- **IP ownership** — states who owns work product after delivery. A typical clause reads: *"All deliverables created specifically for Client under a SOW shall be 'work made for hire' and owned by Client upon full payment; Vendor retains ownership of pre-existing tools, frameworks, and methodologies."* Note the "upon full payment" trigger — ownership transfer is often conditional.
- **Payment terms** — net-30 vs. net-60, invoicing cadence, late-payment interest.
- **Termination rights** — for cause vs. for convenience, and required notice periods.
- **Indemnification** — who covers legal costs if a third party sues over the work.

As a pre-sales professional, you rarely draft the MSA yourself, but you need to know it exists and what it governs, because clients will sometimes ask you scoping questions that are actually MSA questions in disguise (e.g., "what happens if we cancel halfway through?").

| Document | Purpose | Key Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| NDA | Protects confidential information shared during discovery | Confidentiality of both parties |
| MSA | Sets the legal terms governing the overall relationship | Liability, IP, payment, termination |
| SOW | Defines the specific work, deliverables, and price for one engagement | Scope, timeline, acceptance criteria |

{: .important }
> Don't let a client push detailed scope or acceptance criteria into the MSA — that's SOW territory, and blurring the two makes every future project harder to scope cleanly.
