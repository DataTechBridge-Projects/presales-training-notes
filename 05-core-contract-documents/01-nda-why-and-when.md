# NDA – Why and When
{: .no_toc }

*Read duration: 1:07*

A **Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA)** is the document that lets two organizations talk honestly before any money or commitment is on the table. It protects confidential information — client roadmaps, internal architecture, pricing models, customer lists — that either side would never share in an RFP response or a public deck. For pre-sales, the NDA is what unlocks the *real* conversation: instead of a client describing their problem in vague, sanitized terms, they can hand you actual system diagrams, production data samples, or a candid account of why the last vendor failed.

You'll typically encounter two flavors. A **mutual NDA** protects both parties' information and is the default when you and the client are trading sensitive detail in both directions — this is the most common shape in pre-sales discovery, since you may be sharing your own methodology or pricing approach along with hearing theirs. A **unilateral NDA** protects only one side's information, usually the client's, and shows up when a large enterprise buyer insists on a one-way flow of protection because they're disclosing far more than you are.

Timing matters more than most reps realize. The NDA should be signed **before** the first substantive discovery call, not after — if you wait until the proposal stage, you've already had multiple conversations where the client held back detail out of caution, and your scoping suffers for it. A common failure mode is treating the NDA as a formality to rush through; instead, use its signing as the actual gate that opens deeper access to stakeholders and systems.

{: .important }
> A verbal promise of confidentiality is not a substitute for a signed NDA — if a client asks you to "just keep this between us" on a call, that's your cue to get the NDA in place before the next conversation, not a reason to skip it.

Knowing when an NDA is genuinely needed — versus when it's being used to stall — is itself a pre-sales skill you'll sharpen over time.
