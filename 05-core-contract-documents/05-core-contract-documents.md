# Core Contract Documents

Test your understanding of the four core contract documents with the 10 questions below.

**1. What is the primary purpose of an NDA in a pre-sales engagement?**

A. To define the price of the engagement
B. To protect confidential information so both sides can discuss detail openly
C. To cap liability between the parties
D. To document acceptance criteria for deliverables

**2. When should an NDA typically be signed relative to discovery calls?**

A. After the proposal is sent, as a formality
B. Only if the deal closes
C. Before the first substantive discovery call
D. It doesn't matter, as long as it's signed eventually

**3. What distinguishes a mutual NDA from a unilateral NDA?**

A. A mutual NDA has no expiration date
B. A mutual NDA protects both parties' information; a unilateral NDA protects only one side's
C. A unilateral NDA is only used for government contracts
D. There is no meaningful difference

**4. What role does the MSA play relative to individual project SOWs?**

A. It replaces the need for a SOW entirely
B. It sets recurring legal terms once so each project can be scoped through a shorter SOW
C. It defines the specific deliverables for a single project
D. It is only used after a project has already started

**5. Which of the following is a typical MSA clause?**

A. A list of the five dashboards to be delivered
B. Limitation of liability
C. The discovery call agenda
D. The demo script

**6. In an MSA's IP ownership clause, what does an "upon full payment" trigger typically mean?**

A. The vendor keeps the IP indefinitely regardless of payment
B. Ownership of deliverables transfers to the client only once payment is complete
C. The client owns the IP the moment work begins
D. It refers to the NDA, not the MSA

**7. Why is the SOW considered the "most dangerous" of the core contract documents?**

A. It's boilerplate and reused across every client
B. It's bespoke, often finalized under deal pressure, and vague language directly causes scope disputes
C. It never includes pricing information
D. It is signed before the NDA

**8. What makes a SOW deliverable clause defensible rather than dangerous?**

A. Describing the outcome in broad, aspirational language
B. Leaving acceptance criteria to be discussed later
C. Naming countable deliverables, stating exclusions, and capping revision cycles
D. Omitting any mention of what's out of scope

**9. What is a change order?**

A. A verbal agreement to expand scope
B. A formal amendment to the SOW documenting a scope change, its impact, and required approvals
C. A replacement for the MSA
D. An internal delivery team memo that doesn't require client sign-off

**10. What is the most common failure mode that leads to scope creep?**

A. Requiring written approval before any change work begins
B. Delivery teams doing extra work first and raising the change order afterward
C. Turning around impact assessments too quickly
D. Pricing change orders based on the original SOW's rate card

## Answer Key

1. **B** — The NDA exists to protect confidential information so both sides can share real detail during discovery.
2. **C** — Signing before the first substantive discovery call is what unlocks honest, detailed conversation.
3. **B** — Mutual NDAs protect both parties; unilateral NDAs protect only one side, usually the client's.
4. **B** — The MSA sets terms once so each project only needs a shorter, project-specific SOW.
5. **B** — Limitation of liability is a standard MSA clause governing risk exposure between the parties.
6. **B** — Ownership commonly transfers to the client only after full payment is received.
7. **B** — The SOW is custom-written under time pressure, and vague language there directly causes disputes.
8. **C** — Defensible SOW language names specific deliverables, states exclusions, and bounds revision cycles.
9. **B** — A change order is a formal, signed amendment documenting the change, its impact, and approval.
10. **B** — Doing the work before the change order is signed is the classic path into unpaid scope creep.
