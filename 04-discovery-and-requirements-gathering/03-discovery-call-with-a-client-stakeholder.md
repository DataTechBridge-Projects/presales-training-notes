# Discovery Call With a Client Stakeholder

**Scenario:** Priya Nair, a pre-sales solutions consultant, is on a 30-minute discovery call with Mark Feldman, VP of Operations at Northbridge Outfitters, a mid-size specialty retailer. Mark requested the call after seeing a demo of an order management platform at a trade show. Priya is running the **Pain → Impact → Value → Fit** framework.

---

**Priya:** Thanks for the time, Mark. Before I show you anything, I'd rather understand what's actually going on day to day. What made you stop at our booth in the first place?

**Mark:** Honestly, our order management system is falling apart. We're on three different platforms depending on the sales channel — retail stores, our website, and wholesale — and none of them talk to each other.

**Priya:** When you say they don't talk to each other, what does that actually look like on a Tuesday morning for your team?

**Mark:** Inventory counts are wrong half the time. If a store sells the last unit of something, the website doesn't know for maybe four hours, sometimes longer during a sale. We oversell constantly.

**Priya:** How often is "constantly" — daily, weekly?

**Mark:** We're canceling somewhere between 40 and 60 online orders a week because the item shows available but isn't. Customer service handles the cleanup.

**Priya:** That's the pain. Let's put a number on what it's costing you. Fifty cancellations a week — what happens after that, operationally and with the customer?

**Mark:** Each one takes a rep about fifteen minutes to sort out — refund, apology email, sometimes a discount code to keep the customer happy. And we've seen our online repeat-purchase rate drop over the last two quarters. I can't prove it's all this, but the timing lines up.

**Priya:** So roughly twelve to fifteen hours a week of support time on cancellations alone, plus a retention problem you can't fully quantify yet but that's showing up in the numbers. If I'm hearing you right, the real cost isn't just the refunds — it's staff time and a slow leak in customer trust.

**Mark:** When you say it like that, yes. It's worse than I usually frame it internally.

**Priya:** If we fixed the inventory sync so all three channels updated in real time, what would that be worth to you? Not to me — to you, sitting in your seat.

**Mark:** If it cuts cancellations by even 70%, that's maybe ten hours a week back for support, and I'd guess a real bump in repeat purchases. My CFO cares about that repeat-purchase number specifically — it's in her scorecard this year.

**Priya:** Good to know — we'll want her in the loop before this goes much further. Now, on our side: our platform does real-time inventory sync across channels, but I want to be direct with you before we go further. Do you have a hard cutover deadline, like a peak season, that would change how we'd scope this?

**Mark:** We'd want it live before Q4 holiday volume, so end of September at the latest.

**Priya:** That's workable, but tight, and it means your CFO and IT lead need to be part of the next conversation, not a later one. Can you get both of them on a follow-up this week so we scope this against a real deadline instead of a guess?

**Mark:** I can get Denise, our CFO, and our IT director. Let's do Thursday.

**Priya:** Thursday works. I'll come with a rough sync architecture and a couple of clarifying questions on your current tech stack so we're not starting from zero.

---

Notice what Priya never did: she didn't describe a single product feature until Mark had quantified the cancellations, connected them to a metric his CFO tracks, and confirmed a real deadline. By the time Fit comes up, it's a scoping conversation with the right people in the room — not a pitch to a single stakeholder who may not be able to say yes.
