# Role Play 2: Difficult Stakeholder / Executive Pushback

**Scenario:** The follow-up call from the previous role play. Priya Nair now has Mark Feldman on the line along with Denise Cho, CFO of Northbridge Outfitters. Denise is skeptical, time-pressed, and has already sat through two vendor pitches this quarter that went nowhere. She opens by trying to skip straight to pricing.

---

**Denise:** I've got fifteen minutes. Mark tells me you can fix our inventory sync problem. Can you just send over pricing so I can compare it against the other two vendors we're looking at?

**Priya:** I can absolutely get you pricing, and I will — but a number without scope is a number I can't stand behind, and it won't survive your own budget review. Can I ask you two or three questions first? It'll take less time than you think.

**Denise:** Fine, but I've done this dance before. Everyone says "just a few questions" and then it's a forty-minute discovery workshop.

**Priya:** Fair — I'll hold myself to it. Mark walked me through the cancellation numbers last week: roughly fifty a week, about twelve hours of support time, and a repeat-purchase dip you're tracking. From your seat, which of those actually moves the needle for you — the labor cost, or the retention number?

**Denise:** Retention, honestly. Labor cost is real but it's noise next to what we're losing in lifetime value if customers stop trusting the site. But I'll be blunt — I've heard "real-time sync fixes retention" from two other vendors already, and I have no way to know if that's true or just a sales line.

**Priya:** That's a completely fair thing to push on, and I'd rather you push on it now than after we've signed something. Here's the honest answer: sync alone doesn't fix retention — it removes one cause of a bad experience. If your repeat-purchase drop has other causes — shipping delays, pricing, product mix — this won't move that number by itself. Do you have any data on why customers who don't come back say they didn't?

**Denise:** Some exit survey data, yes. Overselling and cancellations show up, but so does shipping time.

**Priya:** Then I'd rather be straight with you: I can solve the overselling piece with confidence. I can't promise it solves your whole retention problem, and anyone who tells you it will is overselling the fix the same way your website's overselling inventory. What I'd suggest is we scope this against the piece we can actually move — cancellations and the support hours tied to them — and treat retention as a metric we watch, not a guarantee we make.

**Denise:** That's more candor than I got from the other two. Okay — but I still need a number before I take this further internally. What's this going to cost, roughly, and what's the timeline risk given we need this before Q4?

**Priya:** Rough range, based on three-channel sync and the deadline Mark mentioned: mid five figures for implementation, plus an annual platform fee I'll detail in writing. The real risk to that September deadline isn't budget, it's your current POS integration — I don't yet know how custom it is, and that's the one thing that could push the timeline. I'd rather find that out in a 45-minute technical session with your IT director this week than guess now and miss the date in August.

**Denise:** That I can work with. I don't love vague numbers, but I respect that you didn't just make one up to keep me happy.

**Priya:** I'd rather lose the deal on an honest number than win it on one I have to walk back in September. Can we get 45 minutes with your IT director by Wednesday, so we're pricing against your actual integration and not an assumption?

**Denise:** Send the invite. And Priya — next time, lead with that "I don't know yet, here's how I'll find out" answer sooner. It's the reason I'm still on this call.

---

The difference from the first call: Denise wasn't going to sit through a slow walk up the framework, and pressing for scope before price would have read as stalling. Priya held the sequence anyway — she just compressed it, named the pushback directly instead of dodging it, and was willing to say "this won't fix everything" rather than oversell **Fit** to close a skeptical executive faster.
