# Opportunity Lifecycle Checklist
{: .no_toc }

This is a working checklist, not a lecture — bookmark this page and come back to it on every live
deal. It's organized around the same nine stages from
[Pre-Sales Lifecycle and Opportunity Stages](../02-pre-sales-fundamentals/02-pre-sales-lifecycle-and-opportunity-stages),
grouped into three moments you'll actually use it: **before** you commit real effort, **while**
the opportunity is open, and **after** it closes. Run down the relevant section whenever you pick
up a new opportunity, and don't move to the next stage's items until the current stage's are
genuinely checked, not just skimmed.

{: .important }
> A stage you compress doesn't disappear — the risk it exists to manage just resurfaces later,
> usually in delivery, where it costs far more to fix. If you're skipping checklist items to save
> time, know which risk you're deferring and to whom.

## Before: Qualification & Intake

- [ ] Confirmed the opportunity has real **budget**, an identified **decision-maker**, and a
      credible **timeline** — not just interest
- [ ] Logged the request (formal RFI/RFQ/RFP or informal ask) and identified which one it actually
      is
- [ ] Made an explicit **go/no-go** call, with a reason, rather than defaulting to "yes" by
      momentum
- [ ] Identified who on your side owns this opportunity (AE, architect, delivery) before work
      starts

**Ask yourself:**
- Do I genuinely believe this is winnable, or am I saying yes because "no" is the uncomfortable
  answer?
- Do I actually have budget, authority, and timeline confirmed — or am I assuming them because the
  client sounded confident?

**Ask the client:**
- Who besides you needs to approve this, and have they been part of this conversation?
- What's driving the timeline — a hard date, or something aspirational?
- Has budget actually been allocated, or is this still a business case you're building
  internally?

## While It's Open

### Discovery

- [ ] Uncovered the client's actual **pain**, not just the requirements they wrote down
- [ ] Quantified **impact** — what the pain costs them in time, money, or risk if left unsolved
- [ ] Connected the solution to **value** the stakeholder can defend internally to their own
      leadership
- [ ] Checked **fit** — confirmed this is a problem your solution genuinely solves, not one you're
      stretching to accommodate

**Ask yourself:**
- Could I explain this client's pain back to them, in their own words, without notes?
- Have I connected it to a number — time, cost, risk — they'd defend in front of their own boss?

**Ask the client:**
- What happens if this isn't solved in the next [their timeframe]?
- What have you already tried, and why didn't it work?
- Who else is affected by this problem that I haven't talked to yet?

### Solutioning & Scoping

- [ ] Written down what's explicitly **in scope** and what's explicitly **out**
- [ ] Listed every assumption the solution depends on, not just the ones that are convenient
- [ ] Had an architect or delivery lead sanity-check the approach before it goes further

**Ask yourself:**
- Could I defend every "in scope" and "out of scope" line to an architect who wasn't in the room?
- Which assumptions here are load-bearing, and have I flagged them explicitly rather than burying
  them?

**Ask the client:**
- Which of these scope boundaries would you push back on, and why?
- Are there systems, data sources, or stakeholders we haven't accounted for yet?

### Estimation & Pricing

- [ ] Attached effort and cost to the agreed scope, not to a rough guess of it
- [ ] Chosen a commercial model (T&M, fixed price, retainer, outcome-based) deliberately, not by
      default
- [ ] Had the numbers reviewed by someone who isn't the one who produced them

**Ask yourself:**
- Would I stand behind this number if I were the one who had to deliver against it?
- Have I priced the risk in this scope, not just the effort?

**Ask the client:**
- What pricing model actually fits how your organization buys — T&M, fixed price, retainer,
  outcome-based?
- Is there a budget ceiling I should know about before I finalize this?

### Proposal / RFx Response

- [ ] Proposal reflects the actual discovery and scoping output — not a template with the client's
      name swapped in
- [ ] Pricing, scope, and value are consistent with each other and with what was discussed verbally
- [ ] Reviewed against likely competitor angles, not just internal completeness

**Ask yourself:**
- Does this proposal read like it was written for this client, or could I swap the logo and reuse
  it?
- If I were the competitor, where would I attack this?

**Ask the client:**
- How will this be evaluated, and by whom?
- What would make this a clear "yes" versus "let's keep shopping"?

### Demo & Evaluation

- [ ] Demo scenario matches the client's real workflow, not a generic canned script
- [ ] Rehearsed the demo against likely objections and technical questions
- [ ] Confirmed what happens if something breaks live — you have a fallback

**Ask yourself:**
- Does this demo show their workflow, or my product's feature list?
- Do I have a fallback ready if something breaks live?

**Ask the client:**
- What does success look like in this demo, from your side?
- Who from your team needs to see this, and are they going to be in the room?

### Negotiation & Contracting

- [ ] NDA, MSA, and SOW terms are aligned with what was actually scoped and priced
- [ ] Any scope defined loosely earlier in the cycle has been tightened up in writing
- [ ] Legal and commercial terms are reviewed by the people who own that risk, not assumed

**Ask yourself:**
- Does the contract match what was actually scoped and priced, or has scope quietly drifted since
  the proposal?
- Is there a verbal commitment anywhere in this deal that isn't written down?

**Ask the client:**
- Is there anything in these terms your legal or procurement team is likely to flag?
- What's your realistic signing timeline, and what could still slow it down?

## After: Closure & Handoff

- [ ] Delivery team has received the full context — assumptions, scope boundaries, client
      history — not just the signed contract
- [ ] Any verbal commitments made during the sales cycle are documented, not left as tribal
      knowledge
- [ ] A named handoff owner on the delivery side has confirmed receipt and understanding
- [ ] Logged what went well and what you'd do differently, while the deal is still fresh

**Ask yourself:**
- Could delivery pick this up cold from what I've handed them, or do they need me in every
  conversation?
- What would I do differently if I ran this deal again?

**Ask the client:**
- Who's the right point of contact on your side going into delivery?
- Are there any commitments made during the sales process that should be captured explicitly
  before delivery starts?
