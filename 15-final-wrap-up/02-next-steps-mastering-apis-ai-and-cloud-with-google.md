# Next Steps — Mastering APIs, AI & Cloud with Google
{: .no_toc }

*Read duration: 1:34*

The pre-sales skills in this guide — discovery, scoping, proposal writing, demo delivery — are durable, but the technical surface area you apply them to keeps shifting. Increasingly, the deals you're qualifying and scoping involve **API management** and **application integration**: a customer wants to expose internal services securely, stitch together SaaS platforms they already own, or build an integration layer that an AI agent can call into safely. If you can only speak to the business outcome and not the technical shape of that request, you'll find yourself deferring every technical question to an SA and losing the thread of the conversation — and often losing credibility along with it.

That's the natural next stretch of study once you've internalized the pre-sales fundamentals: get comfortable with how APIs are designed, secured, versioned, and monetized, and how integration platforms move data and events between systems that were never built to talk to each other. Pair that with working knowledge of the major **cloud platforms** — how compute, identity, and networking decisions show up in a statement of work, and where they quietly drive cost. Layer AI on top of that foundation rather than in isolation from it, because most real AI deployments right now aren't standalone chatbots — they're an AI service wired into a customer's existing systems through an API layer, pulling context from a CRM, writing back to a ticketing system, or orchestrating a multi-step workflow across cloud services.

You don't need to become an engineer to do this well. You need enough fluency to ask the right qualifying questions in discovery, sanity-check an integration approach before it goes into a proposal, and recognize when a demo scenario is technically realistic versus a stretch. Treat APIs, integration, and cloud as the next layer of technical vocabulary to build — the same way you built contract and estimation vocabulary earlier in this guide — and you'll walk into technical conversations as a credible partner instead of a translator standing between the customer and your own technical team.
