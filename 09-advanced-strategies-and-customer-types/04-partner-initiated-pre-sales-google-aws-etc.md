# Partner-Initiated Pre-Sales (Google, AWS, etc.)
{: .no_toc }

*Read duration: 2:36*

Not every deal starts with your own sales team. In a **partner-initiated** opportunity, a cloud provider — Google, AWS, Azure — brings the lead to you, either because their account team identified a customer need that matches your firm's specialization, or because the customer is already committed to that platform and needs an implementation partner to execute. The pre-sales motion is structurally different from a direct deal, and it's worth understanding the shape of it even before you dig into any single provider's specific programs.

The biggest difference is that you're not the only relationship in the room. The cloud provider's account team has its own goals — usually platform consumption and customer retention — that run alongside, and sometimes ahead of, your goal of winning the services engagement. That means your pre-sales work has two audiences: the customer, who needs to trust your solution, and the partner's account team, who needs to trust that recommending you reflects well on them. Scoping calls, discovery, and proposal review often happen with the partner rep present or looped in, which changes how directly you can negotiate and how much internal politics you need to read.

The second difference is funding. Cloud providers run **partner funding programs** — Google's PSF (Partner Services Fund) and PSO (Professional Services Organization) are two examples — that can subsidize a portion of a customer's services cost specifically to accelerate adoption of the platform. These programs exist because the provider benefits more from the customer succeeding on their platform than from any single services engagement, so they'll sometimes fund work a customer wouldn't have budgeted for directly. Knowing these programs exist — and asking the partner rep early whether a deal qualifies — can be the difference between a stalled budget conversation and a funded, fast-moving one.

{: .key-term }
> **Partner-initiated pre-sales** means the opportunity, and often part of the relationship dynamics and budget, originates from the cloud provider rather than from your own outbound or inbound pipeline.

This section only sketches the shape of the topic — the specific mechanics of PSF, PSO, AWS's funding programs, and Azure's equivalents each get their own deep-dive later in the course, with real qualification criteria and case studies.
