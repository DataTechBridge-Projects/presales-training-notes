# Managing Challenging Stakeholders
{: .no_toc }

*Read duration: 2:35*

Every pre-sales rep eventually meets the same handful of difficult stakeholder types, and each one needs a different response — treating them all the same way is what stalls deals. The **ghost** goes quiet mid-cycle, missing calls and letting emails sit unanswered. The **moving target** keeps adding or reshaping requirements after you've already scoped and priced the work. The **skeptical blocker** raises objection after objection, sometimes about the vendor, sometimes about the project itself. And the **over-promiser** — often on your own side, an account executive who told the client something delivery can't actually support — creates a credibility problem you now have to manage.

The common thread across all four is that momentum dies in ambiguity, so the fix is almost always the same instinct applied differently: make things explicit, in writing, with a deadline attached. With a ghost, that means a short recap email after every interaction ("per our call, next step is X, due by [date] — flag if that doesn't work") so silence becomes visibly their delay, not an ambiguous stall. With a moving target, it means routing every new request through your change-order process immediately rather than absorbing it informally — the first time you say "happy to add that, let's scope it as a change order" you reset the norm for the rest of the deal. With a skeptical blocker, it means naming the objection directly rather than talking around it: "it sounds like the real concern is implementation risk, not price — is that fair?" often surfaces the actual blocker in one sentence.

{: .important }
> Never let scope, timeline, or pricing conversations happen only verbally with a stakeholder who has already shown a pattern of unresponsiveness or reversal — get it in writing within the same day, every time.

When a stakeholder genuinely stalls momentum past a reasonable point, escalate deliberately rather than waiting: loop in the economic buyer or an executive sponsor with a factual, non-accusatory summary of what's blocking progress. Escalation done early and calmly reads as professionalism; escalation done late, after frustration has built up, reads as a complaint.
